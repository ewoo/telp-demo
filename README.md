# The Build Sprint — Team 7

> 10 Teams · 5 People Per Team · 2 Hours to Build · 4 Minutes to Demo

**Team:** 7 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Size:** 5 members

---

## The Challenge

Design and build a concept for an **online banking system** that is creative, functional, and fit for a financial services firm — using **Claude Code** as the primary tool.

The goal is *not* production-ready software. It's to demonstrate what's possible when people with domain expertise work in partnership with AI — quickly, deliberately, and responsibly.

### What we're building
- An online banking system — scope, angle, and features are ours to define.
- Think beyond the basics: what makes our solution stand out? What real problem does it solve?
- Consider the full picture: customer experience, back-end logic, risk controls, compliance requirements.
- Domain expertise is the edge — bring it.

---

## How to Approach the Build

**Research → Plan → Implement**

1. **Research** — Use Claude Code to explore the concept before committing. Iterate on ideas, test assumptions, get Claude to push back on our thinking.
2. **Plan** — Map out the architecture, identify risks and gaps, sequence the work. Align the team before writing a line of code.
3. **Implement** — Build it. With a clear concept and solid plan, implementation moves faster and sharper.

> Vibe coding is encouraged — explore freely, try things, see what happens.

## Build Sprint — Managing Our 120 Minutes

| Time | Phase | Team Focus |
|------|-------|-----------|
| 0:00 – 0:10 | Team formation & problem framing | Align on the problem statement. Write it down before prompting. |
| 0:10 – 1:15 | Core build | Focus on the most important feature. Get something working before expanding. |
| 1:15 – 1:30 | Polish & validate | Review output critically. Fix gaps. Make sure it holds up to scrutiny. |
| 1:30 – 2:00 | Prep the demo | Agree on the narrative. Who presents what? What's the headline message? |

### Time management cautions
- **Biggest risk is over-building.** Pick one strong concept and execute well.
- Don't spend more than 10 minutes on any single prompt loop — if it's not working, reframe and move on.
- Reserve at least 5 minutes for demo prep — a great build with a weak presentation costs points.
- Designate one person to watch the clock (call time checks at the 30- and 50-minute marks).

### A note on roles
- No assigned roles — every team member is both a builder and a reviewer.
- Best teams divide and conquer: some prompting Claude Code, others validating output, others shaping the narrative.
- Everyone should be able to speak to what was built and why.

## The Demo — 4 Minutes

Exactly 4 minutes. A signal is given at the 3-minute mark. Over-time presentations are stopped.

**Structure the 4 minutes around three questions:**
1. What did we build, and what problem does it solve?
2. What makes our solution creative, functional, or distinctive?
3. How did our team's expertise shape the design — and what would we build next?

### Demo tips
- Lead with the problem, not the features. Judges want the "why" before the "what".
- Show, don't just tell. Walk through the concept visually.
- Be honest about what Claude Code did vs. what the team decided — that judgment is part of the score.
- Practice handoffs — smooth transitions signal a cohesive team.

---

## Judging Criteria — 100 Points

| Criteria | What Judges Look For | Points |
|----------|---------------------|:------:|
| **Creativity & Innovation** | Goes beyond the obvious? Fresh angle, novel feature, clever use of AI? Points for originality and ambition. | 25 |
| **Functionality & Practicality** | Does it actually work (or credibly would)? Grounded in real-world feasibility? Could it be built and used? | 25 |
| **Risk & Governance Awareness** | Identified and addressed key risks — security, compliance, data privacy, fraud? Sound judgment for a financial services context? | 25 |
| **Presentation & Clarity** | Is the 4-minute demo clear, confident, and well-structured? Does the team explain what, why, and what's next? | 15 |
| **Domain Expertise Integration** | Has the team meaningfully applied expertise from across the firm? Does it feel built by people who understand financial services? | 10 |

> **Note:** Judges weight *what was built* and *how it was presented* equally. A creative, well-explained concept scores higher than a technically ambitious build that can't be clearly communicated.

## Ground Rules
- **Claude Code is the primary tool.** Use it as much or as little as we see fit — but be prepared to explain every decision.
- **No confidential firm data**, client information, or proprietary systems may be used or referenced.
- All team members must be able to speak to the work. Judges may ask questions of any member.
- Whiteboards, notes, and other non-digital planning tools are allowed alongside Claude Code.
- Judging decisions are final.
